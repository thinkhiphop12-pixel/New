What Is a Trequartista?

The playmaker who floats between the lines, creates chances, and barely breaks a sweat doing it — until the moment he decides the game.

Updated 11 August 2026 · BALLKNW

Short answer

A trequartista is an attacking midfielder who operates in the space between the opposition's midfield and defensive lines — the pocket — with almost complete creative freedom. The word is Italian for "three-quarter" (as in the final third of the pitch), and the role is essentially a number 10 with extra flair and fewer defensive responsibilities.

What it actually looks like

Picture a player who drifts wherever he wants in the final third. He's not tied to a flank, he's not stuck in the centre, and he's certainly not tracking back to help the full-back. His job is to find pockets of space, receive the ball between the lines, and create chances for others — or score himself.

The trequartista is the player who makes the game look easy. He plays with his head up, always scanning, always knowing where everyone is. He doesn't run endlessly; he moves intelligently. He conserves energy for the moments that matter, and when he gets the ball, something usually happens.

The trequartista is defined by what he is excused from as much as by what he does. Take away the defensive duty and you get a player permanently available in the one area of the pitch defenders least want to leave.

Trequartista vs number 10 vs playmaker

These terms get used interchangeably and they are not the same thing.

RoleWhat it means
Number 10A shirt number that became synonymous with a position — the attacking midfielder behind the striker(s)
PlaymakerA broader term for anyone who dictates play — could be deep (regista), wide, or advanced
TrequartistaA specific type of advanced playmaker with total creative freedom and minimal defensive work

The trequartista is the most specialised version. He's the luxury player, the one you build the team around because he doesn't do the dirty work. He makes things happen.

Why teams use one

  • To unlock deep defences — when a team sits back, the trequartista finds the gaps between the lines that other players can't see
  • To create numerical advantages — his movement drags defenders out of position, opening space for runners
  • Because you have a special player — not every squad has someone who can play this role. If you do, you use him

The one situation where he struggles is against a genuinely deep defence. A low block has already removed the space between the lines that the role depends on — which is the same reason a false 9 is the wrong answer to a deep block.

The trade-off

The trequartista gives you creativity but costs you defensive solidity. You're essentially playing with ten men out of possession. That's fine if he's good enough to justify it — and if the rest of the team can cover for him.

In practice that means the two midfielders behind him have to be able to hold a midfield on their own, and the full-backs get no protection from in front. Most modern coaches decide the maths doesn't work, which is why the pure trequartista is rarer now than it was twenty years ago.

Famous examples

Roberto Baggio, Francesco Totti, Rui Costa, and more recently players like Paulo Dybala or James Rodríguez in their prime. The role is less common in modern high-pressing systems, but when it works, it's still one of the most beautiful things in football.

Common questions

What is a trequartista in football?

An attacking midfielder who plays between the opposition's midfield and defensive lines with almost complete creative freedom and very little defensive responsibility. The word is Italian for "three-quarter", meaning the final third of the pitch.

What is the difference between a trequartista and a number 10?

A number 10 is the general term for the attacking midfielder behind the striker. A trequartista is the most specialised version of that — more creative licence, more positional freedom, and less defensive work than a typical number 10 is asked to do.

Is a trequartista the same as a playmaker?

No. Playmaker is a broad term for anyone who dictates play, which includes deep-lying playmakers like a regista and wide creators. A trequartista is specifically an advanced playmaker operating in the pocket behind the strikers.

Why is the trequartista rare in modern football?

Because pressing systems ask every attacking player to defend from the front. A player excused from that work leaves the team a man short out of possession, and most coaches would rather have an industrious attacking midfielder than a luxury one.

Who are the best examples of a trequartista?

Roberto Baggio, Francesco Totti and Rui Costa are the classic Italian examples. More recently, Paulo Dybala and James Rodríguez have played versions of the role in their prime.
