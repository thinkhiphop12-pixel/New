What Is Gegenpressing?

The high press turned up to eleven: relentless, coordinated, and absolutely exhausting to play against.

Updated 11 August 2026 · BALLKNW

Short answer

Gegenpress is German for "counter-pressing": the act of pressing immediately after losing the ball, rather than retreating into a defensive shape. The goal is to win the ball back within seconds of losing it, catching the opposition in transition.

What it actually looks like

Imagine a team loses the ball in the opposition's half. Instead of dropping back, every player within range sprints towards the ball. They close down the player who won it, cut off his passing options, and try to win it back immediately.

The logic is simple: the opposition is most vulnerable immediately after winning the ball. They're not organised, they're not in their defensive shape, and they're often facing the wrong way. That's the moment to strike.

Gegenpressing is not just pressing, it's pressing with a specific timing and intensity that makes it uniquely effective. The window is roughly five to ten seconds, and it closes fast.

How it differs from a high press

High pressGegenpress
When it happensWhen the opposition has the ball in their defensive thirdImmediately after your team loses the ball
TriggerOpposition building from the backYour own turnover
IntensityHighExtremely high: immediate and urgent
DurationSustained for phases of playShort, sharp bursts of 5-10 seconds

A high press is a system. Gegenpress is a reaction to losing the ball. Many teams use both.

Why teams use gegenpressing

  • To prevent counter-attacks: the most dangerous moment is right after you lose the ball. Gegenpressing stops the counter before it starts
  • To create chances: winning the ball in transition gives you the best attacking opportunities, because the opposition defence is not yet set
  • To tire the opposition: constant pressing forces quick decisions, and quick decisions produce mistakes

The trade-off

Gegenpressing is physically brutal. Players have to sprint repeatedly, often covering huge distances. It requires a squad with exceptional fitness and the willingness to run. It's also tactically demanding: everyone has to know exactly when and where to press.

And it only works if the team is compact when possession is lost. A side strung out over sixty yards has nobody near the ball to press with, so the counter-press never happens and the counter-attack does.

Famous examples

Jürgen Klopp's teams (Borussia Dortmund and Liverpool) are the most famous exponents. Ralf Rangnick's RB Leipzig, and more recently teams like Brighton under Roberto De Zerbi (in a modified form).

Common questions

What is gegenpressing?

Counter-pressing, pressing immediately after losing possession instead of retreating into shape, in order to win the ball back while the opposition is still disorganised.

What does gegenpressing mean in German?

It literally means "counter-pressing". "Gegen" is German for "against" or "counter", and it is used the same way as in "Gegenangriff", a counter-attack.

How long is the gegenpressing window?

Roughly five to ten seconds after the turnover. After that the opposition has its shape back and the press becomes an ordinary high press against a settled team.

What is the difference between gegenpressing and a high press?

A high press is a planned system for pressuring a team that already has the ball. Gegenpressing is the immediate reaction to your own turnover: same intensity, different trigger.

Why is gegenpressing so tiring?

It requires repeated maximum-effort sprints with no recovery time, on top of the running the team already does in possession. Squads that play this way need deep rotation and exceptional fitness levels.
