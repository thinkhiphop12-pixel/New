What is a 4-3-3 formation?

The formation that dominates modern football: fluid, flexible, and built for possession and pressing.

Updated 11 August 2026 · BALLKNW

Short answer

The 4-3-3 is a formation with four defenders, three midfielders, and three forwards. It's the most popular formation in top-level football today, used by nearly every elite team in some form.

What it actually looks like

A back four. A midfield three: usually one holding midfielder and two more advanced players. A front three: two wide forwards and a central striker.

The key to the 4-3-3 is flexibility. In possession, it can become a 2-3-5, a 3-2-5, or any number of shapes. Out of possession, it can become a 4-5-1 or a 4-1-4-1. The players have to be tactically intelligent and comfortable in multiple roles.

The roles

PositionRole
Holding midfielderSits deep, protects the defence, distributes the ball
Two advanced midfieldersCover ground, link play, arrive in the box
Wide forwardsDrift inside, create chances, score goals
StrikerLeads the line, occupies defenders, scores goals

The two advanced midfielders are usually mezzalas, and the holder is often a regista or a pure destroyer, depending on who else is in the team.

4-3-3 vs 4-2-3-1

These are the two most popular formations in modern football, and they're often confused.

4-3-34-2-3-1
Midfield shapeThree central midfieldersTwo holding midfielders, one number 10
WidthFrom the wide forwardsFrom the wide midfielders
Defensive solidityThe three midfielders can cover the centreTwo holding midfielders protect the defence
Attacking threatThe wide forwards create and scoreThe number 10 creates, the striker scores

Why teams use a 4-3-3

  • Possession: three midfielders give you numerical superiority in the centre
  • Pressing: three forwards can press the opposition back line effectively
  • Flexibility: easy to adapt to different situations
  • Width and central presence: wide forwards give you width, the striker gives you a central threat

The trade-off

The 4-3-3 requires exceptional players. The wide forwards have to be comfortable receiving the ball wide and drifting inside. The midfielders have to cover a lot of ground. It's not a formation for every squad.

It also asks a lot of the full-backs: with the wide forwards tucking in, the only natural width comes from behind them.

Famous examples

Pep Guardiola's Manchester City, Jürgen Klopp's Liverpool, and nearly every top team in Europe. The 4-3-3 is the default formation for modern football's elite.

Common questions

What is a 4-3-3 formation?

Four defenders, three central midfielders and three forwards. It is modern football's default shape because it gives numerical control of midfield and a natural front three for pressing.

What is the difference between 4-3-3 and 4-2-3-1?

The midfield. A 4-3-3 fields three central midfielders in a triangle; a 4-2-3-1 fields two holders and a number 10 ahead of them, with width coming from wide midfielders rather than wide forwards.

Does a 4-3-3 have wingers?

It has wide forwards, which is not quite the same. They start wide but usually drift inside to shoot and combine, leaving the full-backs to provide the width.

What is the weakness of a 4-3-3?

The space behind advancing full-backs, and the workload on the single holding midfielder if both advanced midfielders push forward at once.

Is 4-3-3 good for a possession team?

Yes: three central midfielders create passing triangles all over the middle third, which is why almost every possession-dominant side uses some version of it.
