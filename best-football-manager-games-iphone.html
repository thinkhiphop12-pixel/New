The short answer

The App Store has three real football manager options, and one that skips the App Store entirely. Top Eleven is the social, live-competition heavyweight on iOS. Football Manager Mobile is the paid, tactically serious pick for people who want something close to the real thing. OFM is free-to-play with its own persistent leagues. Gaffa just runs in Safari — no App Store visit, no download, no account, nothing taking up space on your phone.

The four options

Most popular on iOS

Top Eleven (Nordeus)

One of the most-downloaded sports management games on the App Store. You're managing against real people in live leagues, which is the whole draw — but also means it's an ongoing commitment, not a one-off season, and faster progress typically means spending on tokens.

Most realistic, paid

Football Manager Mobile (Sports Interactive / SEGA)

A genuinely scaled-down but still deep version of the real Football Manager, built for touchscreens. It's a paid App Store download — cheaper than the PC version, but still a real purchase, not free.

Free-to-play, persistent leagues

OFM — Online Football Manager (Gameforge)

Free to download from the App Store, built around ongoing competitive leagues against other managers rather than a single season you finish. Like Top Eleven, premium currency speeds things up.

No App Store, no storage used

Gaffa (BALLKNW)

Open it in Safari and it plays like a full game — pick a club across ten leagues, run tactics and transfers, simulate a season — with no App Store download, no iOS storage used, and no account. It's not trying to out-compete Top Eleven's live multiplayer or FM's tactical depth; it's for whenever you want to try club management without adding another app to your phone.

The iOS-specific details

iPhone changes this comparison in ways Android does not, mostly because of how Apple handles payments, web apps, and background activity. Four things are worth knowing before you commit.

App Store pricing and the subscription question

Apple's payment rules mean in-app purchases on iOS are frequently priced a little higher than their Android equivalents, and they make subscriptions and token packs unusually frictionless — a single Face ID confirmation and it is done. That is worth knowing before you start a game built on premium currency. Football Manager Mobile is a straightforward paid App Store purchase with no ongoing cost, which for a heavy player usually works out cheaper than a free game you end up spending in. Top Eleven and OFM are free to download and monetised continuously. Gaffa has no payment path at all — there is no store listing, so there is nothing to buy and no card on file.

Safari, and Add to Home Screen

Browser games on iOS are better than their reputation suggests. Safari's JavaScript engine is genuinely fast on modern iPhones, and Gaffa's simulation runs locally on that engine rather than waiting on a server, so a matchday resolves about as quickly as it would in a native app. Using the Share sheet's Add to Home Screen option gives it a home-screen icon and a full-screen view with no Safari chrome, which is close to the native experience without an App Store account, an Apple ID password, or a download. The catch is specific to iOS and worth planning around: browser-stored saves can be cleared by Safari if you go a long time without opening the site, and clearing website data wipes them immediately.

iPad, and why screen size decides this

If you have an iPad, the ranking shifts. Football Manager Mobile is a substantially better game on a 10-inch screen than a 6-inch one — the attribute tables it inherits from the desktop version stop fighting the display and start being readable, which is most of what makes the game good. Gaffa benefits similarly, with its responsive layout expanding into multi-column views on tablet widths. The two live multiplayer games are largely indifferent to screen size, since their interfaces are fixed-width and phone-shaped either way.

Background behaviour, notifications and battery

iOS is more aggressive than Android about suspending backgrounded apps, which cuts both ways here. Persistent online games like Top Eleven and OFM lean on push notifications to pull you back for live match windows, and those notifications keep arriving whether or not the app is running — manageable, but it is a genuine claim on your attention. Locally-simulated options have no such hook: Football Manager Mobile and Gaffa only do work while you are looking at them, and a Safari tab that is not in the foreground is not consuming battery at all.

Which one actually fits you

You want live competition against other managers

Top Eleven or OFM. Both run persistent leagues against real opponents — accept that they will send push notifications and ask for money.

You want real tactical depth, and ideally an iPad

Football Manager Mobile. A paid App Store title, and the larger screen is what makes its attribute tables genuinely readable.

You just want to manage a club without an App Store trip

Gaffa, straight from Safari. Add it to your Home Screen for a full-screen view with no Apple ID and nothing installed.
