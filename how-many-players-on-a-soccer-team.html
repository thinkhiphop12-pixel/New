How Many Players Are on a Soccer Team?

Eleven on the field. The more useful answer covers rosters, substitutes, and the point at which a match simply stops.

Updated 11 August 2026 · BALLKNW

Short answer

Eleven players per team are on the field at once, including the goalkeeper — 22 players in total. A matchday squad is bigger (usually 18–23 with substitutes), and a club's registered roster bigger again, around 25 senior players.

Soccer or football? Same sport, same number. Soccer and association football are 11 a side either way — only the name changes.

On the field: 11 a side

Law 3 of the Laws of the Game sets it out plainly: a match is played by two teams of not more than eleven players, one of whom must be the goalkeeper. That gives you 22 players on a soccer field during normal play.

Add the officials and the field is busier than the count suggests. A standard professional match has a referee and two assistant referees on the field, with a fourth official on the touchline, so around 25 people are inside the boundary at any moment.

The eleven breaks down as one goalkeeper and ten outfield players. How those ten are distributed between defence, midfield and attack is the formation — 4-4-2, 4-3-3, 3-5-2 and so on. The numbers in a formation always add up to ten, because the goalkeeper is assumed.

The registered figure is where competition rules bite. The Premier League, for instance, caps senior squads at 25 players, of whom at least eight must be home-grown; players under 21 sit outside the cap entirely. That single rule shapes a great deal of transfer business, because signing a 26th senior player means removing someone else first.

How many substitutes are allowed

Five per team in most competitions you will watch. This began as a temporary measure during the congested 2020 fixture calendar and was made permanent in the Laws of the Game 2022/23, which came into force on 1 July 2022. Strictly it is an option rather than a universal rule — the Laws let each competition organiser decide, and most top-level competitions have taken it up.

There is a catch that is easy to miss: the five substitutions must be made across a maximum of three substitution opportunities, with half-time not counting against that limit. Making two changes during the same stoppage uses only one opportunity, so managers tend to swap players in pairs rather than one at a time.

Two further wrinkles apply in the professional game. Unused substitutes and unused opportunities carry over into extra time, and competitions may permit an additional substitution and opportunity on top; separately, most top competitions allow permanent concussion substitutes outside the normal limit. Recreational and youth leagues are far more relaxed — many allow unlimited changes and let substituted players return, which the professional game does not.

The minimum: seven players

A team may not continue with fewer than seven players. Drop below that — through red cards, or injuries once substitutions are exhausted — and the referee abandons the match, with the outcome then decided by the competition's disciplinary process rather than on the field.

In practice this is rare but not unheard of, and it explains behaviour that otherwise looks strange. A team reduced to eight will often abandon any attempt to attack and defend in a deep low block, because a ninth dismissal would end the game entirely.

The reasoning is straightforward: fewer players on a smaller field means every child touches the ball far more often. Dropping eight-year-olds into a full 11v11 game tends to produce a crowd around the ball and several children with nothing to do.

How the 11 are arranged

One goalkeeper and ten outfield players, split across three broad bands. The most common shape in youth and amateur soccer is 4-4-2 — four defenders, four midfielders, two forwards.

  • Goalkeeper (1). The only player permitted to handle the ball, and only inside their own penalty area. Some play as a sweeper-keeper, defending space well outside the box.
  • Defenders (usually 3–5). Centre-backs through the middle, full-backs or wing-backs on the flanks.
  • Midfielders (usually 3–5). Ranging from a defensive holding midfielder to an attacking number 10.
  • Forwards (usually 1–3). A target man, a poacher, or a false 9 who drops into midfield instead of leading the line.

Players are also referred to by number — the 6 is the holding midfielder, the 9 the centre-forward, the 10 the creator. Those numbers describe roles rather than the digits on the shirt, and they are used identically in soccer and football.

Common questions

How many players are on a soccer team?

Eleven per team on the field including the goalkeeper, so 22 in total. A matchday squad is typically 18–23 with substitutes, and a registered senior roster around 25.

How many soccer players are on the field?

22 — eleven from each team, each including a goalkeeper. With the referee and two assistants, around 25 people are on the field during play.

How many substitutes are allowed in soccer?

Five per team in most top-level competitions, made across a maximum of three substitution opportunities plus half-time. It is an option each competition adopts rather than a universal rule. Unused substitutes carry into extra time, where competitions may permit an extra one, and concussion substitutes sit outside the limit. Youth and recreational leagues are often unlimited.

What is the minimum number of players a soccer team needs?

Seven. Below that the referee abandons the match, whether the shortfall comes from red cards, injuries, or both.

How many players are on a youth soccer team?

It varies by age. Commonly 4v4 or 5v5 at under-8, 7v7 at under-10, 9v9 at under-12, and full 11v11 from around under-13.

Is it 11 players in soccer and football?

Yes. Soccer and association football are the same sport, so both field eleven. American football also fields eleven per side, which is coincidence rather than connection.

How many players are on the field in a 7v7 game?

Fourteen — seven per team, normally including a goalkeeper. 7v7 is the standard format for roughly under-9 and under-10 age groups.
