The daily one: Scout

Scout is the game that resets every morning. You get sixteen footballers laid out in a grid and have to sort them into four groups of four, each group sharing something the puzzle does not tell you — the same national team, the same position, the same rating band, the same World Cup squad.

The difficulty is deliberate misdirection. A puzzle will usually include two or three players who look like an obvious group but belong somewhere else, so the set that seems easiest is often the one that costs you a mistake. You get four wrong guesses before the puzzle ends, and a shareable emoji grid at the finish, so you can compare with whoever else played that day.

The Scout guide covers the tactics — which group to solve first, how to spot a decoy, and what the themes tend to be.

Wordle-style vs Connections-style

Daily football games mostly copy one of two puzzle formats, and people search for both under the same names, which causes some confusion.

FormatWhat you doFeedback
Wordle-styleGuess one hidden player within a fixed number of tries.Each guess tells you how close you are — right nation, right club, right age bracket.
Connections-styleSort sixteen players into four hidden groups.You learn only whether a group of four was correct, and you have a small mistake budget.
Grid-styleFill a 3×3 grid where each square needs a player matching two categories.Usually one guess per square, scored on how obscure your answers are.

Scout is the middle one — Connections-style, not Wordle-style. If you specifically want to guess a single hidden player from clues, Scout is not that game, and it is worth saying so plainly rather than letting you find out after clicking.

Common questions

Is there a daily football game like Wordle?

Several exist, in two families. Wordle-style games ask you to guess one hidden player in a set number of tries. Connections-style games give you sixteen players to sort into four hidden groups. Scout is the second kind, with four mistakes allowed and a shareable result grid.

What is a football Connections game?

A daily puzzle showing sixteen footballers to be grouped into four sets of four, each sharing a hidden link — nation, position, rating tier or World Cup squad. The challenge comes from players who plausibly fit more than one group.

Are these football games free?

Yes — all three run in a browser with no account, no download and no payment. Progress saves locally on your device.

Can I play them on a phone?

Yes. All three are built for mobile browsers as well as desktop, with no app to install.

How often do the puzzles change?

Scout has a new puzzle daily. Draft XI and Gaffa are not daily — start a fresh draft or a new season whenever you want.

Do I need an account to save progress?

No. Everything saves to your own browser, which also means clearing your site data will clear your progress.
