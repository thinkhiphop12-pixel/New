Concept — for review

Three takes on a BALLKNW hub built from the same interaction model as the Football Manager 26 console screens: a crest-and-title bar, a grid of self-contained modules, and a persistent dock that always shows what day it is and what the player owes the game next. The structure is borrowed. Everything else — the pitch-black green, the lime-to-emerald ramp, the gold streak accent — is BALLKNW's existing system from theme.css, unchanged. Every screen below is live HTML, not an image, so it reflows at any width and can be lifted straight into the build.

What it borrows

  • Persistent dock. Eight destinations plus the date pod and the action button — the player never loses their place or the clock.
  • Action button carries state. Orange Response required when the game is waiting on you; it flips to green Continue when nothing is owed. Colour is the status, not decoration.
  • Modules are independent. Each card owns its header, its "more" affordance and its own scroll — add or reorder without touching the page.

Where it diverges

  • FM leads with news. BALLKNW leads with the daily Scout puzzle — the one thing that brings people back tomorrow.
  • Star ratings become gold pips, so ability reads at a glance without borrowing FM's iconography.
  • The week strip under the hero doubles as a streak calendar — retention and schedule in one control.

Best for

  • Players deep in a Gaffa save who want the whole club on one screen.

What changes

  • Rail, not dock. Navigation moves to the left edge and frees the full width for content — better on a wide monitor, where a bottom bar wastes the most valuable rows.
  • Tabs name the games. Gaffa, Scout, Draft XI and Perfect Cup sit across the top, so the hub is a home and a switcher.
  • Three-up module grid. Six modules visible without scrolling versus four in Dugout.

What it keeps

  • The date pod and the action button stay pinned bottom-right. That pairing is the single most recognisable thing in the reference — losing it loses the feel.
  • Status still reads as colour before it reads as text: Live now, Tactic unset, Squad picked.

Best for

  • Returning players juggling several games at once — the hub as a dashboard rather than a dugout.

How it compresses

  • The dock swallows the action button. On a phone the CTA sits directly above the nav, inside thumb reach, and the date pod is dropped — a phone already shows the time.
  • Eight destinations become five. Hub, Gaffa, Scout, Trophies, You. The rest live behind the tabs.
  • Modules stack, order is the priority. Today's puzzle, then unfinished games, then your record — nothing below the fold that the player owes.

Same parts, no exceptions

  • Every element here is the desktop component at a phone scale — same tokens, same card, same chips. One system, three densities, so the build shares CSS rather than forking it.

Best for

  • The majority case. Most BALLKNW sessions are one puzzle on a phone, and this screen is built for that visit.

The shared system

Applies to all three

All three directions are the same components at different densities. Colour is doing two separate jobs and they must not be mixed: lime and emerald are the brand, used for identity and the active state; gold, flare and lime-as-success are semantic, and only ever mean streak, action required, and good.

Picking one

The three are not alternatives to argue over in the abstract — they answer different questions about who opens BALLKNW and on what.

  1. If the priority is depth in Gaffa, build Dugout. It is the closest to the reference and the most game-like.
  2. If the priority is players who move between games, build Touchline. The tabs and the rail make the hub a switcher.
  3. Pocket ships either way. It is the same components at phone density, so it is not a fourth decision — it is the responsive form of whichever you pick.

Recommendation: Touchline on desktop, Pocket on mobile, with Dugout's dock kept in reserve for the Gaffa in-save screens where the date pod actually carries meaning.
